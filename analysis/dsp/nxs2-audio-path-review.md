# NXS2 audio path review (2026-09-24)

Reference: Stijn-Jacobs/cdj-nxs2-qemu at `1c7818f8a290dc1cef3b08207c01eb851d2d80fc`, especially `hw/cdj/dsp_audio.c` and `hw/cdj/c6x/c66x_core.md`.

## What can transfer

The NXS2 model passes genuine C6655 McBSP0 transmit words to a QEMU audio voice. Its firmware produces 44.1 kHz stereo, left-aligned 24-bit PCM words in left/right order. The sink converts each word to signed 16-bit, queues frames in a bounded ring, waits for a prefill level after underrun, drops old frames to limit latency, and optionally adjusts the read rate by up to 4% for host-clock drift. These are useful **sink design** choices once our transmitted words and sample clock are verified. The ring and host callback are downstream of the guest bus, so they cannot repair an absent DSP signal.

Our C674x sends through McASP1 and McASP2, with EDMA feeding XBUF. `cdj_c6747_mcasp_tx_slot()` moves accepted XBUF words to XRSR; the default functional audio mode calls it once per 1024 DSP packets and can capture those genuine words. This packet interval is explicitly coarse and has no physical sample timing. A connected PLAY capture below now verifies `word >> 16` and alternating left/right slots for the TESTTONE.WAV fixture on McASP1. It does not verify output pacing or host audio.

The observed firmware configuration in `runs/agent-play-checkpoints/main-stderr.log` provides a strong lead: McASP1 has one enabled transmit serializer (0), two TDM slots (`AFSXCTL=0x113`), 32-bit slots (`XFMT=0x180f0`), internal high/bit clocks (`AHCLKXCTL=0x8000`, `ACLKXCTL=0xe5`), and `XTDM=3`. With the board's modeled 16.9344 MHz AUXCLK, the existing clock query derives a **44.1 kHz stereo frame rate**: 16,934,400 / (6 bit-clock divider × 32 bits × 2 slots). The resulting slot rate is 88.2 kHz. This identifies McASP1 serializer 0 as the first candidate for a host PCM sink. McASP2 is configured for DIT (`DITCTL=1`, `AFSXCTL=0xc002`) on serializer 3, so its mapping should be investigated separately as digital output. The 44.1 kHz calculation does not establish that current DSP scheduling produces 44,100 frames per virtual second.

## Connected PLAY evidence and the failed-run cause

The 33 transmit captures present before this review were scanned: **1,318,618 genuine XBUF records, zero nonzero words**. Those captures predate the most recent playback fix or cover idle/replay states. The later native playback fixture in `runs/agent-play-sdram-fixed` shows nonzero PCM reaching DSP SDRAM, and `runs/agent-play-checkpoints` shows four records briefly consumed after the active-low REV contact was corrected. That run then stalled because EDMA completion acknowledgements were lost; `analysis/dsp/native-play-edma-completion.md` documents the fix. The subsequent `runs/agent-play-edma-fixed` attempt did not capture McASP TX. The older zero-only captures were therefore inconclusive about connected playback.

A bounded replay of the real post-REV checkpoint 7 with the current EDMA model, functional timing and TX capture ran for 22 million additional packets without a fault. It recorded 42,968 more serializer words, all zero. The stream descriptor did not move because this isolated replay had no later MAIN/HPI events, so it does not test whether a new connected PLAY run would consume and transmit nonzero records. Its artifacts are `/tmp/cdj-audio-checkpoint7-replay` and `/tmp/cdj-audio-checkpoint7-cont` on the investigation host.

`tools/cdj_dsp/tx_capture.py` reports nonzero counts by instance/serializer and the first nonzero word in the capture metadata. This makes a fresh run's first useful output easy to identify without treating arbitrary serializer words as PCM.

A new opt-in `--capture-dsp-tx-nonzero-only` diagnostic retains only nonzero genuine XBUF words, so silent boot cannot exhaust the capture bound before PLAY. It is deliberately a detection aid: its record sequence is contiguous for **retained** words, and it cannot measure underruns or prove sample timing. The first attempt with ordinary bounded capture, `runs/agent-audio-live-20260924`, filled its 200,000-record limit during boot. Three subsequent nonzero-only runs (`runs/agent-audio-nonzero-20260924`, `runs/agent-audio-live-fixed-20260924`, and `runs/agent-audio-browse-20260924`) retained zero words but never reached a browser row or PLAY. They were **media-path failures, not negative audio evidence**. In the first nonzero-only run, an incorrectly relative QMP endpoint prevented the readiness worker from pressing SOURCE; the launcher now records an absolute socket path.

Two conditions explain the repeated NO CARD stall. The prior successful `runs/agent-play-checkpoints` used `--fresh-link`; the stalled runs did not. The successful run's MAIN link queue stayed at depth zero across 5,017 deliveries, while the failed runs held at depth 63 for 94.7% and 98.4% of deliveries. Fresh-only delivery restored the empty-queue behavior in `runs/agent-audio-fresh-20260924` and is now the NXS default; `--cached-link` retains the earlier behavior for comparison. Separately, the launcher's SD source-key worker used the broad mount gate. Its recorded press in that run happened at mode 2/table 0/latch 1, and the browser answered NO CARD. After virtual time advanced past 300 seconds, a new SD press produced a TESTTONE.WAV row. The source-key worker now waits for mode 3/table 2/latch 1 with a present card, while the historical mount-gate `ok` result remains available to `wait-media`.

In `runs/agent-audio-fresh-20260924`, two real ENTER presses loaded the 10-second fixture (`duration_field=2560`). With active-low REV released, `wait-playback` observed the native counter advance five frames. The run's `dsp-tx.jsonl` (SHA-256 `b2883b37feb8955ccdfb5ebc9dba300959381c060121b73ef2121d6d67f3b4c8`) contains 100,000 retained nonzero genuine XBUF records: 50,000 McASP1 serializer 0 and 50,000 McASP2 serializer 3. McASP1 alternates slot 0 and slot 1, 25,000 of each. All 25,000 left words converted by `word >> 16` match the fixture's first 25,000 **nonzero** left-channel samples in order; every retained right word matches the corresponding left word, consistent with the fixture's duplicated mono tone. McASP2 words match McASP1 word for word. The first retained word is `0x00800000`, or signed-16 value 128, following the WAV's initial zero frame. The nonzero-only capture omitted 22 zero words within this segment. Filling those positions from `xbuf_sequence` reconstructs **25,011 complete stereo frames** whose bytes exactly match the source WAV starting at frame 1. `mcasp1-reconstructed.wav` in that run is a listenable 0.567-second excerpt, labeled 44.1 kHz from the source format. This proves the accepted word order for that segment, but the packet-based scheduler still cannot establish a 44.1 kHz delivery rate or host playback. It does establish that the native firmware, EDMA, and McASP path produces correct PCM data during PLAY.

The next integration step is to drive McASP1 slot advancement from the existing OSCIN/AUXCLK timebase rather than 1024 DSP packets, then capture **all** slot words during PLAY to measure silence, underruns and continuity. A host ring with prefill, bounded latency and explicit underrun metrics is a sensible output stage after that rate check. The present emulator has working PCM generation but no verified live speaker path.

The exact excerpt can be reproduced with `python -m tools.cdj_dsp.tx_to_wav runs/agent-audio-fresh-20260924/dsp-tx.jsonl /tmp/mcasp1.wav --rate 44100`. The exporter validates the capture, requires McASP1 serializer 0 stereo slot order, reconstructs zeros from XBUF sequence gaps, and labels the WAV with a caller-supplied rate. It is an offline listening tool, not a live output sink or a rate measurement.

QEMU captures now include `virtual_ns` beside packets and cycles; replay captures omit it because replay has no QEMU virtual clock. A 30-wall-second cold boot probe, `runs/agent-audio-clock-probe-20260924`, retained 500 genuine zero McASP1 words over 93.320 ms of QEMU virtual time. The 499 word intervals imply **5,347 accepted words/s, or about 2,674 stereo frames/s**, in this short idle-boot window. That is 16.5 times below the configured 88.2 kHz slot clock. This does not measure a PLAY segment, but it rules out attaching a 44.1 kHz host voice to the current 1,024-packet slot scheduler and expecting it to stay fed. The DSP `pll.oscin_cycles` counter also advances only when the interpreter executes, so substituting it directly would not model an independently running McASP clock. Slot scheduling needs a separate virtual-time event source and a bounded strategy for servicing EDMA/DSP between events.

An opt-in `--virtual-mcasp-clock` now batches slots from the configured McASP1 frame clock at 1 ms QEMU virtual-time intervals. It requires `--functional-dsp-audio` and limits each synchronous DSP interpreter activation to 4,096 packets, allowing QEMU to service timer callbacks between slices. McASP2 still advances alongside McASP1 as a functional DIT approximation. A first version that resumed the DSP only after McASP activation never reached the firmware's McASP setup; the timer now also services DSP work before activation. A 30-second probe with the corrected mode (`runs/agent-audio-virtual-sliced2-20260924`) booted without DSP fault and captured genuine zero XBUF words. A longer bounded idle capture (`runs/agent-audio-virtual-long-20260924`) recorded 50,000 McASP1 serializer words over 564.549 ms of QEMU virtual time, or **88,565 words/s** versus the 88,200 word/s derived from the configured 44.1 kHz stereo clock. McASP2 yielded a similar rate. Capture timestamps are read during each callback and therefore include batch processing time; this 0.4% difference is a diagnostic measurement, not verified serializer edge timing. Both runs occurred before a connected PLAY action. The new mode is explicitly excluded from architectural timing validation and has no host output.

The opt-in `--host-dsp-audio-wav` adds a QEMU WAV voice to that mode. It accepts every McASP1 serializer 0 slot 0/1 word, including repeats when XBUF is not refreshed, converts its upper 16 bits to interleaved signed stereo samples, and queues frames in a bounded two-second ring with 50 ms prefill after underrun. A dedicated QEMU shutdown notifier closes the backend so the WAV header is finalized when the launcher stops MAIN. In `runs/agent-host-audio-wav-slot-20260924`, Python's WAV reader accepted the output as 44.1 kHz, 16-bit stereo with 220,409 frames. The stream was all zero because the run remained at idle boot. QEMU reported 165,805 DSP frames queued, 163,506 read, no underruns and no drops; the extra output is prefill/boot silence. This establishes the host sink connection and valid file output, but **does not yet establish nonzero connected PLAY audio or audible device playback**. The NXS2 ring/prefill idea transferred, while its old `AUD_*` calls had to be adapted to this QEMU checkout's `audio_be_*` interface.

The first connected virtual-clock attempt, `runs/agent-host-audio-play-20260924`, reached SD mode 3/table 2, listed `TESTTONE.WAV`, and accepted two ENTER presses. Its command-5 load reply retained a zero duration, however, and the deck displayed **“Media or File is collapsed or unavailable”** with **E-7010 DSP DEVICE ERROR**. No nonzero genuine XBUF record was captured. The finalized host WAV contains 15,161,180 stereo frames, all zero; QEMU reported no host-ring drop or underrun, and no C674x interpreter fault was logged. This is a failed connected PLAY test, not evidence that the WAV sink works during playback. Over 344 wall seconds the interpreter retired 1.203 billion packets and counted 2.676 billion modeled cycles, roughly 3.50 million packets and 7.78 million cycles per wall second. The failure is upstream of nonzero host audio; likely candidates include guest-visible EDMA/McASP event interleaving and insufficient DSP interpreter progress while the independent McASP clock advances. Neither cause is proven by this run. The run's binary predates the later change that feeds repeated XRSR slots to the host ring; that change cannot manufacture a missing genuine nonzero XBUF transfer.

A follow-up 420-second connected experiment, `runs/agent-virtual-interleave-play-20260924`, split the existing 4,096-packet timer-callback budget across its McASP slots so the DSP could observe EDMA events between slot advances. The run completed without a C674x interpreter fault, but regressed before the previous load failure: the SD readiness worker still saw mode 0/table 0/latch 0 at shutdown, no browser row or load response appeared, and the final screen showed **E-8709 COMMUNICATION ERROR**. Its 18,578,496-frame host WAV and genuine-XBUF capture contained no nonzero sample. The GUI received no link payloads, while the earlier connected run received 274,392 bytes by 340 seconds. The experiment therefore does not validate interleaving as a fix; it was reverted. Its exact failure mechanism remains unproven. Routine DSP report throttling was changed independently to use elapsed virtual time, preventing fine-grained scheduling experiments from multiplying checkpoint output.

The opt-in `--render-dsp-audio-wav` records every McASP1 serializer-0 slot pair directly to a WAV as the DSP advances, independent of the QEMU host audio callback. It requires `--functional-dsp-audio`, checks the firmware's two-slot 44.1 kHz configuration, and labels the file with that nominal rate. The packet scheduler remains coarse; this is a sample-sequence render, not a real-time clock or speaker path. A 30-second idle smoke run produced a valid stereo WAV with 84,333 silent frames. In the connected `runs/agent-dsp-render-play-20260925` run, the SD browser listed `TESTTONE.WAV`, command 5 replied with duration field 2560, the native playback counter advanced five frames, and 100,000 retained nonzero genuine-XBUF records filled the capture bound. The finalized `dsp-render.wav` contains 1,517,438 stereo frames, 97,582 with at least one nonzero channel. After 1,415,917 leading idle frames, its next **94,051 consecutive frames exactly match** source `TESTTONE.WAV` starting at source frame 1; both byte slices have SHA-256 `5d6f520d4d8b8eb41960ced50b4e8ee94c6c3f631b4ef633ef30cf57c7c4c5d4`. `dsp-render-listen.wav` in that run is the exact 2.13-second matching excerpt without boot silence. The remainder of the short connected tail contains silence gaps, so the test does not establish uninterrupted full-track output or real-time playback. The connected binary predates a later change that only adds a nonzero-frame diagnostic counter.

`runs/agent-virtual-three-enter-20260925` repeated the virtual-clock/host-WAV test with the **same SD image** as the successful DSP-paced run and the same three ENTER presses. The second and third command-5 replies still had zero duration, the GUI displayed E-7010, and both the direct and host WAVs stayed silent. Its genuine-XBUF capture was empty, with no interpreter fault. This rules out the missing third ENTER press as the earlier failure's explanation. At shutdown the virtual clock had produced 9,365,399 stereo frames while the interpreter had retired 1,607,373,201 modeled cycles, at most **172 modeled DSP cycles per frame** including boot work. The firmware's PLL writes (`PLLM=0x16`, `POSTDIV=0x8000`) and the model's enabled SYSCLK1 divider yield 389.4912 MHz, or **8,832 DSP cycles per 44.1 kHz frame**. Thus this host-paced experiment supplies at most about 2% of the DSP progress implied by the configured clock. The guest-visible McASP clock is running far ahead of the interpreted DSP. This is strong evidence of timing starvation, although it does not isolate every cause of the zero-duration reply. A timing-faithful live sink needs DSP and McASP to advance against one virtual clock; increasing a host ring or adding another ENTER cannot supply the missing DSP cycles.

## C66x core approach

Their C66x core uses table-driven decode, packet-shape caching, operand masks, explicit pipeline write latencies and cross-path stalls, SPLOOP iteration overlays, and a fixed-point detector that skips a proven idle poll loop until the next event. Those are worthwhile **architectural and performance ideas** for a measured C674x bottleneck. Their report says the idle loop is a fixed point on 39,564 of 40,019 passes; the skip is guarded by unchanged registers, no external stores/bus activity, no TSC read, no interrupt, and no pending writes. A similarly guarded idle optimization could reduce our host cost without fabricating audio.

The C66x decoder/opcodes and McBSP device cannot be transplanted to the C674x/McASP board. Our strict C674x path still has unresolved SPLOOPD epilog timing and functional packet-based McASP scheduling. Replacing that with their core would lose our board-specific peripheral behavior and checkpoint contracts. A better course is to validate a shared instruction subset with differential traces and consider a cache of decoded immutable packets, with invalidation on CPU and DMA writes to executable memory. First measure whether decode or event scheduling dominates a representative native PLAY run; copying their fast path before profiling has no demonstrated benefit.
